# Provider readiness research (historical)

The permission-review activation gate described below was superseded on 2026-09-30. Configured providers are now enabled or disabled through Admin; the base configuration remains the media-host allowlist and adapter inventory.

**Reviewed:** 2026-09-28  
**Scope:** the local provider input selected by `PLAYBACK_MEDIA_CONFIG` in the local `apps/api/.env`, read only from the ignored [`infra/playback/providers.local.json`](../infra/playback/providers.local.json), plus the corresponding adapter implementations. This is not a snapshot of deployed Postgres provider overrides or production configuration.

## Finding

The local file has **62 configured rows, 35 enabled rows, and 32 distinct enabled adapter identities**. Its enabled rows resolve to **11 `direct`, 2 `direct-preferred`, and 22 `proxy-required`** delivery modes. The `direct`/`proxy-required` labels describe routing only; they do not establish media rights. The counts were read from the local JSON and checked against the provider adapter map in [`audit-active-providers.mjs`](../scripts/audit-active-providers.mjs).

**No active identity has a permission basis documented in the reviewed repository materials or established by the first-party materials reviewed here.** Where an official source page or API exists, it generally documents a player, embed, or multi-source resolver; that is not evidence of a license for Consumit to resolve media URLs and deliver them directly or through its proxy. Where first-party material could not be located or retrieved, permission is **unknown**, not presumed. This is a readiness finding, not a legal determination.

The first-party disclosures that do exist make the gate more urgent: VixSrc says its catalog is scraped from multiple websites and promotes custom embed domains to bypass ISP blocks/avoid detection; 123anime and Anikai say they do not host files and use non-affiliated third-party sources; Kyren's terms limit its use license to personal, non-commercial use and prohibit public display, mirroring, and reverse engineering; Rive publishes torrent and aggregator endpoints. None of those statements grants Consumit a service-level distribution right. [VixSrc](https://vixsrc.to/), [123anime](https://123anime.la/), [Anikai](https://animekai.stream/), [Kyren Terms](https://kyren.moe/terms), [Rive API docs](https://www.rivestream.app/embed/docs)

## Coverage boundary and next review

This pass mapped **all 32 enabled adapter identities and all 35 enabled row IDs** from the local file. It reviewed retrievable first-party pages for 23 identities (including shared adapter families); it did not establish a license for any. Exact identities still lacking a retrievable, relevant first-party API/terms source are: **`anibd`, `aphrodite`, `lookmovie`, `modiplay`, `nineanime`, `poseidon`, `vidapi`, `wecollege`, and `zoko`**. Some of these have a reachable product page but no relevant API or rights documentation; they remain in the list because the needed evidence is still missing.

Continuation checklist:

1. For each enabled row, obtain an agreement or provider-issued policy that names Consumit and expressly covers the specific works, territories, commercial/service use, direct URL resolution, browser playback, and any proxy/CDN relay. An iframe integration guide alone is insufficient evidence for a different delivery path.
2. Confirm the rights chain for every separate origin/CDN and any aggregator or scraper behind the adapter. Record the accountable licensor, supported title kinds/territories, delivery mode, term, and revocation contact against the provider row.
3. Keep rows with unknown or restrictive permission out of production until the evidence is approved and recorded. Playback probes and successful adapter resolution remain technical health evidence only.
4. Re-run this audit against the deployed provider configuration and database overrides before release; the ignored local file does not prove what production serves.

## Runtime enforcement

The API provider-configuration seam now requires an approved `permissionReview` record before an enabled row can enter the playback registry. The record captures the evidence basis and URLs, reviewer and timestamp, public-service and commercial-use scope, regions, media kinds, and direct/proxy-fallback modes. Missing reviews are logged and skipped; a record narrower than the row's configured scope fails startup. This makes the reviewed local configuration the activation boundary; it does not independently verify evidence URLs or establish legal rights. The current local snapshot has no such records, so it intentionally activates no configured providers until review is completed.
